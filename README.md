 1. Executive Summary
This report analyses 2,500 admissions across 8 clinical departments over 2024–2025. The hospital recorded a 12.28% 30-day readmission rate (307 cases), an average length of stay (LOS) of 3.8 days, and a bed occupancy rate of 8.8% against a 150-bed capacity. Mortality stood at 3.64% (91 cases). Total billed revenue was ₹5.85 Cr, equivalent to ₹23,386 per case and ₹6,076.1 per patient-day.
The sections below examine the quarterly trend in admissions and readmissions, length-of-stay and discharge-outcome distributions, department- and doctor-level readmission risk, and revenue drivers by insurance type and department. Recommendations focus on the departments and doctors most in need of clinical-quality follow-up.

7. Recommendations
Clinical Quality
●	Prioritise a discharge-planning review in Neurology, the department with the highest readmission rate and among the longer average stays.
●	Review case-mix and discharge protocols for DOC-006 and the other four flagged doctors; benchmark against Orthopedics' care pathway, the best-performing department on both readmission and revenue efficiency.
Patient Follow-Up
●	Build a structured follow-up program for the 35-49 and 65+ age bands, which show the highest readmission risk.
●	Investigate the root causes behind the 5.8% "Left Against Advice" cohort — this group is likely to be over-represented in future readmissions.
Capacity Planning
●	At 8.8% occupancy against 150 beds, there is meaningful headroom for elective volume growth without compromising emergency capacity.
Revenue
●	Continue balanced payer-mix strategy; no single insurance category exceeds 26% of revenue, which limits reimbursement-policy risk.

Methodology Note
Figures are computed directly from the 2,500-row admission-level dataset underlying the accompanying Power BI dashboard (hospital_ops_dashboard). Bed occupancy = total patient-days ÷ (150 beds × 730 days). Readmission rate reflects the ra flag among all admissions. Doctor-level breakdown applies a minimum sample size of 20 cases to avoid small-sample distortion; readmission rate by doctor may reflect case-mix differences and should be read as a screening signal, not a definitive quality score.
